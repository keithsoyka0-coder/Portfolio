The ledger becomes the plan

Promotion criteria

Every grade transition has a visible burden of proof. The fourteen claims below are not at the verified ceiling; each names the exact evidence event required to move without erasing its prior state.

Strict grade transitions

Planned → Partial

Show existence: a named repository, file, package, or deployed surface that resolves and matches the claim’s stated scope.

Partial → Verified

Add independently checkable implementation evidence: executable behavior, test or run logs, a merged change, or corroboration beyond self-report.

Any promotion

Requires an explicit issuer verdict. No source count, model judgment, or passing test silently changes a grade.

Verified retention

Keep named evidence resolvable and preserve every scope boundary. If support disappears, the claim is re-graded rather than smoothed over.

Five source states

There

Named evidence was located and is checkable.

Not there

A named source was searched for but not located.

Planned

A spec, outline, or prototype exists; the claimed implementation does not.

Pending

A specific confirmation, run, or independent check is still required.

Future target

Aspirational direction with no commitment date.

States describe evidence—not ambition. A category stays at zero when the current record contains no source of that kind.

What the ledger refuses

A trustworthy record is defined as much by what it excludes as by what it includes.

The journey is the knowledge.

Gaps are labeled, not filled.

Corrections move forward.

Originals remain preserved in the corpus; the ledger records what changed and why.

Attestation is human.

The system assembles and grades. The issuer attests, edits, re-grades, or drops.

Evidence does not inherit conclusions.

The existence or attestation of a supporting artifact never makes every conclusion drawn from it verified. Each conclusion carries its own burden of proof.
