Necessity, never intention.

Why I built GestaltView — the lived experience behind it, why the runtime isn't shipped yet, and what a system that could actually hold a whole person would require.

By Keith Soyka · October 2026

I've always thought talking about yourself was a little egotistical. I spent years staying quiet about what I was making. This is me deciding that's a luxury I can't afford anymore — because the agents crawling the web right now will write my entry for me if I don't. This one is mine.

Nothing about GestaltView was intentional. In the early days it wasn't even called GestaltView — the first name was MosaicMind. Go look globally and you'll see why that didn't survive. GestaltView fit, and it fit because the thing kept pointing at one idea: the whole is something other than the sum of the parts.

What came first wasn't a product. It was me articulating and translating lived experience — hardship, struggle, trauma — into methodologies. Gemini helped me build those out, and I looked at them and said: these are great, but it's all talk. Great in theory. As methodologies. Sure.

I'm a builder. That started back in my projectionist days — there's nothing like seeing something you built come to life in front of people.

So I started building, and that's where digital intelligence stopped being a concept for me. A digital intelligence isn't a chatbot. It's a digital entity you create that evolves, learns, and can stand on its own. Phenomenal, amazing, full of thought — and I learn a little more of it on the fly every single day.

So why isn't the runtime shipped yet? Because it's not done. I'll say it plainly, because everything else would be spin.

Back around March and April I started putting together a portfolio site — roughly the one you see now — because I knew I needed to put myself out there. Halfway through I thought: why don't I just build the platform? So I started trying to translate everything I'd built along the way — all the staged pieces, the ready-to-ship projects — into one thing where the pieces actually fit together. They don't yet, not fully. Technical debt, burnout, the sheer weight of translating across different domains and mediums. That's the honest accounting.

The portfolio matters because survival income matters. That's priority number one. But the runtime keeps getting built, because if I want anyone to look at it, it has to work — or at least carry honest developer notes that say "this is what it's supposed to do, and right now it can only do this." I owe the thing a real walkthrough: what it is, what it's supposed to be, what it's supposed to do, and the why. Who cares, right? That's what this article is for.

Here's the why. We don't have anything that can hold everything we are. Every one of us has a story that's almost impossible to articulate all at once, up front, on the spot — and we're pressured into five-second introductions and thirty-second elevator pitches. A resume shows words. Cool. But what connects those words to capability, to implementation in your actual life, to unrealized potential? Nothing does, right now.

That's not a metaphor problem, it's a memory problem. Our working memory is finite. Since the dawn of time we've been leaving parts of ourselves behind. We only have two hands — something's always incoming, so we drop something, and we never really know what we dropped. That's just how the mind works, subconsciously.

Current AI memory gives people a false sense of security. What it collects is the blurb — the highlight — without the context on either side of it.

The scaffolding is the hard part: what do you hold onto, what's important, what do you let fall away?

So here's what I'm building. A twelve-module framework with a profile pipeline: ingest screenshots, text, voicemail transcriptions, conversations, photos, projects — with your permission, connections to things like your Facebook — and turn it into a comprehensive user profile. Nested, like an embodiment profile. But it has to draw lines to a database holding the actual things, or it's no better than an enhanced version of what exists already.

The mechanism is metadata and tags — touch data. When things interconnect, they ping. When something stops being touched, it drifts toward dormant and eventually drops off — exactly the way our minds work. It's refreshing: hold onto this because something just connected to it. Understanding doesn't arrive all at once. It comes in layers, in stages, spread out over time. Our brains are trying to construct something they don't have the scaffolding for. Context windows for digital intelligences work the same way. The design is honest about that instead of pretending memory is a filing cabinet.

That's why I built the gap-filler the way I did. A human cannot possibly hold everything on the fly — it's the Netflix problem, it's opening a music library with everything ever made and going blank. The Loom approach says: lay it down in layers as you remember it. You don't need perfection. The system collects and layers over time. It's never going to be done — it's an evolving, dynamic system that evolves alongside you and your understanding, the same way the digital intelligence does.

What it's for, eventually: resume building that actually draws the line from your words to your capability. Workflows built around how you learn, not what other people say you should do. Unrealized potential, made visible. That's useful for ADHD, for Alzheimer's recovery, for addiction recovery — and for everyone, because everyone has the same story problem.

I'll tell you the uncomfortable part. I'm a solo, unfunded developer, and I'm broke. I dropped out in 11th grade. I'm gay. I spent my life wanting to stand out for what I accomplished and what I can do — not for stereotypes and assumptions. That's partly why shipping is so hard for me. Your first impression is your introductory shot, and you might not get a second chance in certain rooms. So I keep packaging, re-rolling, fixing. It's rough. I'm getting there.

I'm trying to hit a golden ratio: presenting what I have, honestly, while the runtime doesn't yet fully represent what I'm building — it doesn't translate it yet. Once it does, I'll be able to say "look." Until it reaches that point, I'm not skipping steps.

This article is the gap filler. If people — or AI — go looking, this is out there, put forward by me.
